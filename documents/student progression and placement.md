# Student Progression & Placement Policy

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy defines how Common Ground determines students' initial levels, monitors their academic development, and decides when they are ready to progress to the next level.

It covers:

* placement;
* diagnostic assessment;
* level determination;
* academic progression;
* uneven performance across language skills;
* remaining at the same level;
* extraordinary assessment;
* level transfers.

This document complements the **Academic Policy**, **Assessment Policy**, and **Attendance & Make-up Policy**. It does not replace the procedures described in those documents.

---

## 2. CEFR Levels

Common Ground organizes its English courses according to the following CEFR levels:

* A1
* A2
* B1
* B2
* C1

Students are placed according to their demonstrated ability rather than simply according to their previous course history.

A student's level represents their overall current proficiency. Performance may vary across individual language skills.

---

## 3. Placement

### 3.1 Definition

**Placement** is the process of determining which level a student should enter.

Placement takes place before a student begins a course or when an existing student needs their level to be reassessed.

Placement is intended to place students in a learning environment that is appropriate for their current proficiency and allows meaningful academic development.

### 3.2 Placement Components

Placement may include:

* a written or online diagnostic assessment;
* a speaking activity or interview;
* reading and listening tasks;
* writing tasks;
* questions about the student's previous learning experience;
* additional evidence when the initial results are unclear.

The exact assessment instruments may vary according to the student's profile and the information available.

### 3.3 Placement Is Not a Final Evaluation

Placement is different from course assessment.

A placement decision determines the most appropriate starting level. It does not constitute a final grade and does not determine whether a student has completed a level.

---

## 4. Diagnostic Assessment

Diagnostic assessment is used to identify a student's current strengths, difficulties, and learning needs.

The diagnostic process may consider:

* speaking;
* writing;
* listening;
* reading;
* interaction;
* communicative effectiveness;
* language control appropriate to the student's level.

Diagnostic assessment should provide enough information to support an informed placement decision.

When the results from different components suggest different levels, the student's overall profile should be considered rather than relying on a single score or task.

---

## 5. Level Determination

Level determination is based on the student's demonstrated performance and the CEFR level descriptors used by Common Ground.

Academic staff may consider:

1. results from placement activities;
2. diagnostic evidence;
3. performance across different language skills;
4. previous academic records when available;
5. teacher or coordinator observations when relevant.

A previous course level does not automatically determine a student's new placement.

When the evidence is inconclusive, Academic Coordination may request additional assessment before confirming the placement.

---

## 6. Progression

### 6.1 Definition

**Progression** is the process of determining whether a student is ready to move from their current level to the next level.

Progression is based on evidence accumulated throughout the course rather than on a single classroom performance.

### 6.2 Progression Criteria

A student may progress when they:

* demonstrate the expected learning outcomes for their current level;
* meet the academic performance requirements established by the Assessment Policy;
* meet the attendance requirements established by the Attendance & Make-up Policy;
* demonstrate sufficient overall communicative competence for the next level;
* have completed the required course assessments.

Teacher feedback and evidence from formative and summative assessment may contribute to the progression decision.

Meeting one criterion does not automatically guarantee progression when other required criteria have not been met.

### 6.3 Progression Between Levels

Progression normally follows the sequence:

**A1 → A2 → B1 → B2 → C1**

Students should not be moved to a higher level solely because they perform well in one isolated activity.

Accelerated progression may be considered when sufficient evidence demonstrates that the student is prepared for the higher level.

---

## 7. Uneven Performance

Students may demonstrate different levels of ability across speaking, writing, listening, reading, and interaction.

For example, a student may demonstrate B2-level speaking while their writing remains closer to B1.

Uneven performance does not automatically prevent progression.

When performance differs significantly across skills, the decision should consider:

* the student's overall communicative profile;
* the learning outcomes of the current level;
* the severity and relevance of the weaker area;
* evidence from multiple assessments;
* teacher feedback;
* the student's ability to participate successfully in the next level.

A student may progress with a documented area for continued development when the overall evidence indicates readiness for the next level.

When a weaker skill represents a substantial barrier to successful participation in the next level, the student may remain at the current level or receive targeted academic support.

---

## 8. Remaining at the Same Level

A student may remain at the same level when the available evidence indicates that additional development is necessary before progression.

Reasons may include:

* insufficient achievement of the current level's learning outcomes;
* significant gaps in one or more language skills;
* insufficient evidence to confirm readiness for the next level;
* unsuccessful required assessment;
* failure to meet another progression requirement.

Remaining at the same level is not considered a disciplinary measure.

The student should receive information about the areas that require further development and, when appropriate, recommendations for additional practice or academic support.

---

## 9. Extraordinary Assessment

An **extraordinary assessment** is an additional assessment opportunity used when the standard assessment process does not provide sufficient evidence to determine a student's academic status or when an exceptional academic circumstance justifies further evaluation.

An extraordinary assessment may be considered when:

* a student's results are inconclusive;
* there is substantial disagreement between assessment evidence;
* a student has demonstrated significant development after an unsuccessful assessment;
* an exceptional circumstance has affected the student's ability to demonstrate their proficiency.

Extraordinary assessment is not an automatic right to repeat or replace a failed assessment.

The request should be reviewed by the teacher and, when necessary, Academic Coordination.

The format and criteria of an extraordinary assessment must follow the principles established in the Assessment Policy.

---

## 10. Level Transfer

A level transfer is a change in the student's assigned academic level after enrollment.

A transfer may be considered when:

* new evidence shows that the student's current placement is inappropriate;
* the student's proficiency has changed significantly;
* a placement or diagnostic decision needs to be reviewed;
* an academic review indicates that the student would benefit from another level.

Level transfers should be based on documented academic evidence whenever possible.

Teachers may recommend a level transfer, but the final decision follows the school's academic procedures and may require approval from Academic Coordination.

A level transfer should be communicated clearly to the student, including the reason for the decision and any relevant academic recommendations.

---

## 11. Roles and Responsibilities

### Teachers

Teachers are responsible for:

* monitoring student development;
* providing formative feedback;
* documenting relevant academic observations;
* identifying possible progression or placement concerns;
* communicating concerns to Academic Coordination when necessary.

### Academic Coordination

Academic Coordination is responsible for:

* supporting complex placement decisions;
* reviewing cases involving unclear or conflicting evidence;
* approving level transfers when required;
* reviewing requests for extraordinary assessment;
* ensuring that progression decisions follow the school's academic policies.

### Students

Students are responsible for:

* participating in assessment activities;
* providing accurate information during placement;
* engaging with feedback;
* communicating relevant academic concerns;
* following the procedures established by Common Ground.
