
# Common Ground Assessment & Feedback Framework

**Version:** 1.0
**Audience:** Students, Teachers, and Academic Staff
**Document type:** Assessment and Feedback Policy

---

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how learning is assessed at Common Ground.

Assessment is used to:

* monitor student progress;
* identify strengths and areas for development;
* provide actionable feedback;
* support decisions about progression;
* encourage learner autonomy.

Assessment should support learning rather than function only as a final measurement of performance.

For general academic rules and course organization, see the **Academic Policy**.

---

## 2. Assessment Philosophy

Common Ground evaluates students primarily through their ability to use English effectively in meaningful contexts.

Assessment values:

* communicative competence;
* intelligibility;
* interaction;
* contextual appropriateness;
* clarity;
* autonomy;
* effective use of language.

Accuracy is considered important, but it is not the only measure of successful communication.

Common Ground does not require students to reproduce a particular native-speaker accent or variety of English. English is understood as a diverse international language and a means of communication among people with different linguistic and cultural backgrounds.

---

## 3. Formative Assessment

Formative assessment occurs throughout the learning process.

Teachers may use:

* classroom observation;
* short tasks;
* discussions;
* written activities;
* projects;
* self-assessment;
* peer feedback;
* informal checks of understanding.

The purpose is to identify what students can already do and what they need to develop.

Formative assessment should inform subsequent teaching and learning activities.

---

## 4. Summative Assessment

Summative assessment evaluates student performance at the end of a relevant learning period.

It may contribute to decisions about:

* completion of a module;
* final academic results;
* progression to another level.

Specific progression criteria are defined in the **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.

---

# 5. Assessment Components

Assessment may consider the following areas.

## 5.1 Speaking

Speaking assessment considers:

* fluency;
* interaction;
* intelligibility;
* ability to communicate meaning;
* contextual appropriateness.

Pronunciation should be evaluated primarily in relation to intelligibility rather than conformity to a specific accent.

## 5.2 Writing

Writing assessment considers:

* clarity;
* organization;
* adequacy for the intended purpose;
* linguistic control;
* ability to communicate meaning effectively.

## 5.3 Listening

Listening assessment considers the student's ability to:

* understand relevant information;
* identify key ideas;
* interpret meaning in context;
* respond appropriately to spoken English.

## 5.4 Reading

Reading assessment considers:

* comprehension;
* identification of relevant information;
* interpretation;
* inference;
* understanding of meaning in context.

## 5.5 Participation

Participation may be considered when it is relevant to the learning objective.

Meaningful participation includes:

* contributing to activities;
* interacting with classmates;
* responding to others;
* collaborating;
* taking responsibility for learning tasks.

Participation is not simply the amount of time a student speaks.

## 5.6 Projects

Projects may be assessed according to:

* achievement of the task objective;
* communication;
* process;
* collaboration;
* final product;
* ability to present or explain the outcome.

---

# 6. Assessment Criteria

Assessment criteria should be communicated clearly before significant assessed activities.

Teachers should consider:

* the learning objectives;
* the student's demonstrated performance;
* the context of the task;
* the relevant language skills.

A single mistake should not normally determine a student's overall performance.

Assessment should consider patterns of performance and evidence collected over time when appropriate.

---

# 7. Grading and Progression

When numerical grades are used, the grading criteria and minimum requirements should be communicated to students as part of the relevant course or assessment.

A final result may consider multiple assessment components rather than a single activity.

Assessment results may contribute to progression decisions, but **assessment and progression are not identical processes**.

The criteria and procedures for moving between CEFR levels are defined in the **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.

---

# 8. Feedback

Feedback should help students understand:

1. what they are doing well;
2. what needs improvement;
3. what they can do next.

Effective feedback should be:

* specific;
* relevant;
* understandable;
* actionable;
* respectful.

Feedback may be:

* individual;
* collective;
* written;
* spoken;
* provided during or after an activity.

The appropriate timing of corrective feedback depends on the objective of the activity. The **Teacher Handbook** provides detailed guidance on feedback and error correction during lessons.

---

# 9. Self-Assessment

Students should have opportunities to reflect on their own learning.

Self-assessment may involve:

* identifying strengths;
* identifying difficulties;
* reviewing learning goals;
* evaluating progress;
* defining next steps.

Self-assessment supports learner autonomy but does not replace formal assessment.

---

# 10. Peer Feedback

Peer feedback may be used when appropriate to the learning objective.

Students should receive:

* clear criteria;
* instructions about respectful feedback;
* guidance on focusing on the task and learning objectives.

Peer feedback supports reflection and interaction but does not replace the teacher's academic assessment.

---

# 11. Recovery and Additional Assessment

A recovery or additional assessment may be considered when a student has not demonstrated the required learning outcomes or when available evidence is insufficient to make an academic decision.

Additional assessment may also be appropriate when there is significant uncertainty about a student's demonstrated level or performance.

Requests for extraordinary assessment should follow the procedures established in the **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.

---

# 12. Final Results

Final academic results should be based on the applicable assessment criteria and evidence collected during the relevant learning period.

Students should receive clear information about their results and, when appropriate, feedback explaining their performance and recommended next steps.

Questions about progression, level changes, or exceptional academic decisions should be directed according to the procedures established in the **Academic Policy** and **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.
