# Common Ground Academic Policy

**Version:** 1.0
**Audience:** Students, Teachers, and Academic Staff
**Document type:** Institutional Academic Policy

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the general academic rules and procedures of Common Ground.

It defines how the school's courses are organized, how students enroll and change levels, how academic responsibilities are divided, and how academic decisions are handled.

This policy applies to all Common Ground students, teachers, and academic staff.

Specific procedures are described in the following related documents:

* **Teacher Handbook:** teaching practices, pedagogical principles, and teacher procedures.
* **Assessment & Feedback Framework:** assessment philosophy, criteria, feed back, and grading.
* **Student Progression & Placement Policy:** placement, level determination, and progression between levels.
* **Attendance & Make-up Policy:** attendance, absences, and make-up procedures.

When a specific policy provides a more detailed rule, that policy should be consulted instead of repeating the procedure here.

---

# 2. Course Structure

## 2.1 Levels

Common Ground offers English courses organized according to the following CEFR-aligned levels:

* A1 — Beginner
* A2 — Elementary
* B1 — Intermediate
* B2 — Upper-Intermediate
* C1 — Advanced

CEFR levels provide a general reference for language proficiency. Placement and progression decisions are governed by the **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.

## 2.2 Course Modules

Courses are organized into modules designed around specific learning objectives.

A module may combine:

* language development;
* communicative practice;
* receptive skills;
* productive skills;
* projects or tasks;
* formative assessment.

The exact sequence and activities may vary according to the course and learner needs.

## 2.3 Class Organization

Classes may be organized according to:

* proficiency level;
* course objective;
* schedule;
* student availability;
* pedagogical considerations.

The Academic Coordination team may reorganize classes when necessary to maintain appropriate learning conditions.

---

# 3. Enrollment

## 3.1 Enrollment Process

Enrollment confirms a student's place in a Common Ground course.

The standard process includes:

1. selecting an available course;
2. completing the enrollment process;
3. completing placement when required;
4. receiving enrollment confirmation;
5. receiving information about the class schedule and course procedures.

## 3.2 Placement Before Enrollment

Students with previous English-learning experience may be required to complete a placement process before being assigned to a level.

Placement procedures are described in the **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.

## 3.3 Changes After Enrollment

Requests to change classes, schedules, or levels should be submitted to Academic Coordination.

Changes are subject to:

* available places;
* compatibility with the student's current level;
* course structure;
* academic considerations.

Teachers should not independently approve permanent transfers between levels or classes.

---

# 4. Level Changes

A student's level should reflect their demonstrated ability to participate successfully in the academic activities associated with that level.

Level changes may occur through:

* initial placement;
* regular progression;
* additional assessment;
* approved transfer between classes or levels.

Placement and progression decisions should consider evidence from multiple language skills rather than relying exclusively on one performance or isolated activity.

The detailed criteria and procedures are established in the **Student Progression & Placement Policy**.

Teachers who believe that a student's current level may not be appropriate should document relevant evidence and consult Academic Coordination.

---

# 5. Withdrawal and Cancellation

## 5.1 Withdrawal

Students who wish to pause or discontinue their studies should follow the applicable administrative procedure communicated by the school.

Withdrawal may affect:

* access to classes;
* academic records;
* future enrollment;
* progression within a course.

## 5.2 Cancellation

Cancellation refers to the termination of an enrollment according to the applicable institutional or contractual conditions.

The academic consequences of cancellation depend on the student's circumstances and the stage of the course.

Where financial or contractual issues are involved, the appropriate administrative policy takes precedence over this academic policy.

## 5.3 Return After Withdrawal

Students returning after a period away may be required to undergo a new placement or academic review when there is uncertainty about their current level.

Academic Coordination determines the appropriate procedure.

---

# 6. Student Responsibilities

Students are responsible for actively participating in their learning.

Academic responsibilities include:

* attending classes according to the applicable attendance policy;
* participating in learning activities;
* completing required assessments and assignments;
* communicating relevant academic difficulties;
* reviewing and using feedback;
* respecting classmates and teachers;
* following institutional procedures.

The **Attendance & Make-up Policy** establishes the specific attendance requirements and procedures for absences.

The **Assessment & Feedback Framework** establishes assessment-related responsibilities.

---

# 7. School Responsibilities

Common Ground is responsible for providing an academic environment consistent with its stated educational principles.

The school's academic responsibilities include:

* providing scheduled classes;
* communicating relevant academic information;
* applying assessment procedures consistently;
* maintaining academic records;
* providing appropriate feedback;
* monitoring student progress;
* providing mechanisms for academic support;
* communicating significant academic decisions.

Specific teaching practices and classroom responsibilities are described in the **Teacher Handbook**.

Assessment procedures are described in the **Assessment & Feedback Framework**.

---

# 8. Academic Procedures and Decision-Making

## 8.1 Academic Coordination

Academic Coordination is responsible for decisions that require institutional authority or coordination between different academic areas.

Examples include:

* level changes;
* exceptional progression decisions;
* extraordinary assessments;
* permanent class transfers;
* interpretation of academic policies;
* recurring academic concerns;
* exceptional academic circumstances.

Teachers should not make institutional exceptions independently.

## 8.2 When Teachers Should Escalate a Situation

A teacher should contact Academic Coordination when:

* a student's academic difficulty persists;
* the student's appropriate level is unclear;
* multiple policies apply to the same situation;
* an exception may be necessary;
* additional assessment may be appropriate;
* the situation exceeds normal classroom responsibilities.

The **Teacher Handbook** provides the practical procedure for documenting and communicating these situations.

## 8.3 Evidence-Based Decisions

Academic decisions should be based on relevant evidence whenever possible.

Evidence may include:

* assessment results;
* student work;
* classroom observations;
* attendance records;
* documented feedback;
* evidence of progress over time.

A single classroom incident or isolated performance should not normally determine a student's academic status.

---

# 9. Academic Records

Common Ground maintains academic records necessary to support teaching, assessment, progression, and institutional decision-making.

Records may include:

* enrollment information;
* placement results;
* assessment results;
* attendance;
* relevant feedback;
* progression decisions;
* documented academic interventions.

Teachers should record information according to the procedures established by the school.

The Teacher Handbook provides guidance on what teachers should record and how to communicate academic concerns.

---

# 10. Academic Support

When a student experiences academic difficulty, Common Ground may provide appropriate support based on the nature of the difficulty.

Possible actions include:

* additional feedback;
* learning recommendations;
* adapted classroom activities;
* additional practice;
* academic monitoring;
* additional assessment;
* referral to Academic Coordination.

Support should be based on the student's demonstrated needs rather than assumptions about their ability.

# 12. General Principles

Academic decisions at Common Ground should follow these principles:

1. **Transparency:** students should receive clear information about relevant academic procedures.
2. **Consistency:** comparable academic situations should be handled according to the same institutional criteria.
3. **Evidence:** decisions should consider relevant academic evidence.
4. **Communication:** students and staff should know when and how to seek academic support.
5. **Flexibility:** reasonable adaptations may be considered when appropriate.
6. **Student development:** academic procedures should support learning and progression rather than exist solely as administrative controls.
